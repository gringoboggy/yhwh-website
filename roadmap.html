The road so far — and ahead

An honest account of where the work stands. There are no dates and no promises here — only what has been built, what is being shaped now, and what we hope to add in time. The program is given freely at whatever stage it has reached; nothing below is held back behind a price, and nothing below is owed to you on a schedule.

Development stages

  1. Shipped

    The storehouse & study apparatus

    One 87-book Ethiopian Tewahedo superset — the widest scriptural canon in continuous use — with 91,733 study notes gathered into one place and organized into families you can turn on or off. The deepest free study apparatus we could assemble, stored once, in plain text.

  2. Shipped

    The customizable Bible builder

    Choose your canon and tradition, toggle the notes you want, add original-language verse popups (Hebrew, Greek, Latin, and Arabic), design your cover and title pages, and export a clean EPUB 3 — validated to zero errors and zero warnings. Nine starting editions build today, entirely on your own computer.

  3. In progress

    A standalone Geʽez Bible from the manuscripts

    Transcribing a complete Bible in classical Geʽez directly from the original manuscripts, witness by witness, with a faithful English back-translation trailing each book. This is slow, careful, reverent work. 1 and 2 Samuel are complete; the books of Kings are underway.

  4. Opening soon

    The first public release (Beta)

    Preparing the first public beta of the desktop program — free, complete, and open for everyone to try, with feedback warmly wanted. See the beta page →

With your support, what comes next

These are hopes, not commitments — undated, and conditional on the time and support to reach them. They are listed so you can see where the work would go, not so anyone is owed them.

  • All the manuscripts, rendered online — so the original pages can be read freely by anyone, not only transcribed into the text.
  • A standalone Amharic Bible — alongside the Geʽez, in its own right.
  • More public-domain source texts — deepening the storehouse further.
  • Public-domain audio — curated human-narrated readings (from LibriVox), embedded natively in the ebooks.
  • Broader reading plans — more ways to journey through the text.

These are undated hopes, not scheduled features. Some may arrive sooner, some later, and some may change. Whatever comes, the program stays completely free — a sponsorship helps keep it alive and growing (it pays for development time, manuscript digitization, and the hosting and domain for this site), but it never buys anyone a feature others don’t get.
